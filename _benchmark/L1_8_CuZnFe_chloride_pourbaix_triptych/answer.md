## Answer

**Widest soluble chloro-complex window — copper wins, uniquely.** Only Cu opens a genuine chloro-complex predominance field in 0.1 M Cl⁻ at 1 mM metal. The Cu(I) dichloro-cuprate **[CuCl₂]⁻** occupies a quasi-rectangular box spanning **pH 0 → ~6.46 and E ≈ +0.097 → +0.381 V (SHE)** — an area of roughly 6.5 pH units × 0.28 V. Zinc's ZnCl⁺ and ZnCl₂(aq) never overtake Zn²⁺ (fractional shares ~25 % and ~4 %), and iron's FeCl⁺, FeCl²⁺, FeCl₂⁺ likewise never dominate — no Zn–Cl or Fe–Cl region appears anywhere on either map. Chemically, this is a chloride/soft-acid affinity story: Cu(I) is a borderline-soft cation for which chloride is an excellent σ-donor, so β₂ for [CuCl₂]⁻ (log β₂ = +6.06) is large enough that at [Cl⁻]=0.1 M it collapses free Cu(I) activity by ~6 log units and pulls the Cu(0)/Cu(I) couple down from the bare +0.52 V to ~+0.10 V, carving out a wide predominance box. Zn(II) and Fe(II)/Fe(III) are hard, oxophilic cations — their chloride log β values (+0.4/+0.6 for Zn; −0.20/+0.78/+2.13 for Fe) are simply too small to beat aqua-ion or hydroxo/oxide competitors at 0.1 M Cl⁻; you would need multi-molar brines to move them.

**Order of first hydroxide/oxide precipitation along E = +0.2 V (low → high pH):**

1. **Fe — α-Fe₂O₃ (hematite) at pH ≈ 4.02** (bracketed 4.019–4.031). Ferric iron is a very hard, small +3 cation with strongly acidic aqua ligands (pKa₁ ≈ 2–3), so Fe(III) hydrolyses and condenses to a ferric oxide almost as soon as one leaves the strongly acidic corner — the +0.2 V line lies above the Fe²⁺/Fe(III) redox boundary, so it crosses directly from Fe²⁺(aq) into hematite.
2. **Cu — Cu₂O (cuprite) at pH ≈ 6.46–6.47** (grid-resolved). At +0.2 V copper is in its Cu(I) window, and the boundary is essentially vertical: 2 [CuCl₂]⁻ + H₂O ⇌ Cu₂O(s) + 4 Cl⁻ + 2 H⁺. Chloride actually *raises* this onset relative to a Cl-free Cu(I) system by stabilising [CuCl₂]⁻, but hydroxide finally wins at near-neutral pH.
3. **Zn — ZnO (inactive) at pH ≈ 6.59** (bracketed 6.581–6.594). Zn²⁺ + H₂O ⇌ ZnO(s) + 2 H⁺; the boundary is pH-only (both sides Zn(II)) so it holds at any E ≥ −0.86 V, including +0.2 V. Zn(II)'s slightly weaker hydrolysis than Cu(I)-in-chloride puts its onset just above copper's.

So on the +0.2 V isopotential the passivation sequence is **Fe (pH ~4.0) « Cu (pH ~6.46) < Zn (pH ~6.59)**. Iron precipitates a full ~2.5 pH units earlier than the other two because +0.2 V is already oxidising enough to force it into Fe(III), and Fe(III) is aggressively hydrolytic; Cu and Zn are within ~0.15 pH units of each other, reflecting comparable second-hydrolysis pKa's of the Cu(I)/Cl⁻ pool and free Zn²⁺.
