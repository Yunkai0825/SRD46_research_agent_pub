## Answer

At fixed Eh = +0.265 V vs SHE with 1.00 mM Cu(total) and 0.100 M total ammonia, copper walks through seven distinct regimes as pH climbs from 0 to 14. The chemistry is governed by two competing gradients: NH4+ deprotonates near pH 9.3 to release the free NH3 that Cu(II) needs to form ammine complexes, while OH− grows monotonically and eventually out-competes NH3 at the metal centre. Because the applied Eh sits right on the Cu(II)/Cu(I) boundary near neutral pH, a Cu(I) "island" (Cu2O and [Cu(NH3)2]+) opens up exactly where free NH3 first becomes available but is still too scarce to stabilise the Cu(II) ammine ladder.

Working up the pH axis:

- **pH 0–5.6 — free Cu²⁺(aq).** Ammonia is fully locked up as NH4+ (free [NH3] ~ 10⁻⁹ M), so the aquo ion is the only accessible form. Cu²⁺ peaks at 98.7 % at pH 0.
- **pH 5.6–7.0 — Cu2O(s) (cuprite).** Hydrolysis of Cu²⁺ combined with the fixed Eh (+0.265 V) drops Cu into the Cu(I) oxide; peak 72 % at pH 6.2.
- **pH 7.0–8.2 — [Cu(NH3)2]+.** Rising [NH3] pulls Cu(I) back into solution as the linear diammine (log β = +9.92); peak 65.4 % at pH 7.2.
- **pH 8.2–8.3 — narrow [Cu(NH3)3]²+ window.** Free NH3 has now grown enough that the Cu(II) ammine ladder switches on and oxidatively out-competes Cu(I) at the fixed Eh.
- **pH 8.3–10.5 — [Cu(NH3)4]²+ tetraammine.** The classic deep-blue complex dominates (peak 90.8 % at pH 10.2); log β4 = +12.3 combined with [NH3] ≈ 0.1 M swamps hydrolysis.
- **pH 10.5–13.4 — Cu(OH)2(s).** OH⁻ activity keeps growing while the ammine ladder is saturated at four NH3, so hydroxide displaces the tetraammine and Cu precipitates (peak 99.5 % at pH 11.7).
- **pH 13.4–14 — CuO2²⁻ (cuprate).** Amphoteric redissolution as the doubly-deprotonated cuprate ion; HCuO2⁻ appears as a minor 13.9 % shoulder at pH 13.5.

**Practical implications.** Total dissolved Cu is suppressed by two precipitation windows (Cu2O at pH 5.3–7.2 and Cu(OH)2 at pH 10.3–13.5). To keep Cu fully solubilised as the deep-blue tetraammine, the sweet spot is pH ≈ 9.5–10.3 with excess ammonia — acidifying protonates NH3 away and alkalising loses Cu to hydroxide. The Cu(I) sub-region between pH 5.5 and 8.2 is a direct fingerprint of the chosen Eh: at higher Eh it would collapse into Cu(II) throughout; at lower Eh it would broaden and possibly reach metallic Cu.
