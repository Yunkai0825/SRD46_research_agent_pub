Stability window of the glycinate complexes

  • NiGly+ predominates from pH 5.8 to 6.8 (peak 61 % at pH 6.3); NiGly2 from pH 6.8 to 8.3 (peak 71 % at pH 7.6); NiGly3− from pH 8.3 to 10.9 (94 % at pH 10.5).
  • Below pH ≈ 5.8 the ligand is protonated (HGly zwitterion, pKa 9.57) and free Ni2+ takes over; above pH 10.86 crystalline β-Ni(OH)2 becomes saturated at 1 mM Ni (≈ 11.7 if the hydroxide is freshly precipitated, dotted line).
  • Lower potential limit is nickel deposition: −0.35 V at pH 5.8, −0.38 V at pH 7, −0.45 V at pH 8.3, −0.55 V at pH 10, −0.56 V at pH 10.9 (vs SHE). Complexation lowers the Ni/Ni(II) line by 30, 59 and 89 mV per pH unit in the NiGly+, NiGly2 and NiGly3− fields until glycine is fully deprotonated near pH 9.6.
  • Upper limit is oxidation to β-NiOOH: +1.15 V at pH 5.8, +1.00 V at pH 7, +0.92 V at pH 8.3, +0.80 V at pH 10, +0.67 V at pH 10.9. This line lies above the O2/H2O line throughout the window, so the complexes are stable over the whole water stability field between pH 5.8 and 10.9.
