Joint Cu–Fe E–pH predominance diagram

1 mM Cu(II) + 1 mM Fe(III) in 10 mM citrate, 10 mM glycine, 0.1 M chloride and 0.1 M total ammonia, 25 °C, I = 0.1 m. Full mass-balance speciation solved at every point of a 701 × 626 grid (ΔpH = 0.02, ΔE = 4 mV); each cell is coloured by the species that holds the largest share of the metal, so solid/solution boundaries are 50 %-precipitated lines and aqueous boundaries are equal-concentration lines. Hydrated solids only: Cu(OH)₂, Cu₂O, Fe(OH)₃(am), Fe(OH)₂ (Fe₂O₃, FeOOH, Fe₃O₄, CuO excluded as requested).

How the two metals partition

Copper

  • Cu(II) (top of the map): free Cu²⁺ only below pH 3.2 (with ≈20 % CuCl⁺); CuCit⁻ from pH 3.2 to 5.9; Cu(Gly)₂ from pH 5.9 to 11.5 — glycinate beats the tetra-ammine (Cu(NH₃)₄²⁺ never exceeds ≈1 % even at pH 10–11, because log β₂(Gly) = 15.0 at 10 mM glycinate outweighs log β₄(NH₃) = 11.75 at 0.1 M NH₃); Cu(OH)₂(s) precipitates from pH 11.5 and redissolves as Cu(OH)₄²⁻ above pH 13.5.
  • Cu(I) has two separate stability windows: CuCl₂⁻ (≈79 % CuCl₂⁻ + 20 % CuCl₃²⁻) between +0.12 and +0.33 V for pH 0–6.0, and Cu(NH₃)₂⁺ between ≈0 and −0.20 V for pH 7.0–11.5; in between (pH 6.0–7.0) Cu(I) disproportionates, except for a ≈10 mV Cu₂O(s) sliver (pH 6.2–7.0). Cu₂O is the Cu(I) phase above pH 11.3.
  • Cu(s) deposits below +0.12 V at pH ≤ 5, +0.05 V at pH 7, −0.15 V at pH 9, −0.20 V at pH 11 and −0.31 V at pH 13 — always well above the hydrogen line.

Iron

  • Fe(III) is citrate-bound above pH 1.2: FeCit(aq) (pH 1.2–2.3), then the bis-citrate family [Fe(Hcit)₂]³⁻ (2.3–3.6), [Fe(cit)(Hcit)]⁴⁻ (3.6–5.95) and the doubly alkoxide-deprotonated [Fe(cit)₂]⁵⁻ (5.95–14). With fresh Fe(OH)₃(am) (log *Ks0 = 4.89) no ferric-hydroxide region exists: 10 mM citrate keeps 1 mM Fe(III) dissolved to pH 14, where Fe(OH)₄⁻ reaches 50 %.
  • Fe(II) is far less stabilised: free Fe²⁺ to pH 3.9 (≈5 % FeCl⁺), FeCit⁻ pH 3.9–9.1, a narrow Fe(Gly)₂ strip (9.1–9.9), then Fe(OH)₂(s) above pH 9.9.
  • Because citrate binds Fe(III) ~10¹⁰–10²⁵ times more strongly than Fe(II), the Fe(III)/Fe(II) line falls from +0.74 V (pH < 1.2) to +0.17 V at pH 3.6, −0.16 V at pH 6, −0.55 V at pH 9 and stays at ≈ −0.59 V ([Fe(cit)₂]⁵⁻/Fe(OH)₂) for pH 10–14. The Fe(II) window therefore narrows from 0.46 V at pH 6 to ≈0.06 V at pH 9.8 and re-widens to 0.3 V at pH 14 once Fe(OH)₂(s) takes over.
  • Fe(s) needs −0.56 V (pH ≤ 4) to −0.90 V (pH 14): 0.07–0.56 V below the hydrogen line everywhere (closest to it at pH 9–14).

Assumptions, sensitivity and caveats

Ferric citrate model. The result that Fe(OH)₃ never precipitates rests on the bis-citrate family with alkoxide deprotonation (Vukosav, Mlakar & Tomišić; converted to the Cit³⁻ basis with the citrate alcohol pKa = 14.4 of Silva, Kong & Hider) together with a fresh hydrous ferric oxide. Sensitivity run with an aged hydroxide (log *Ks0 = 3.2 instead of 4.89): Fe³⁺ (pH 0.00–1.16) → FeCit(aq) = [Fe(Hcit)] (pH 1.18–2.28) → [Fe(Hcit)₂]³⁻ (pH 2.30–3.60) → [Fe(cit)(Hcit)]⁴⁻ (pH 3.62–5.94) → [Fe(cit)₂]⁵⁻ (pH 5.96–13.10) → Fe(OH)₃(s) (pH 13.12–14.00). Older 1:1 models (FeCitH₋₁⁻, log β ≈ 9) would bring Fe(OH)₃ in around pH 8–9. Di- and trinuclear ferric citrates are omitted; they matter at Fe:citrate ≈ 1:1, not at the 1:10 ratio here.

Other omissions. Ternary Cu(Gly)(NH₃)ₙ complexes (no critically evaluated constants at I = 0.1) — they could take part of the Cu(Gly)₂ field at pH 9–11 without moving the Cu(II)/Cu(I)/Cu(0) lines much. Cu(II)-citrate dimers and Cu₂(OH)₂²⁺ (≤ 5 % of Cu at 1 mM). Fe(III)–ammonia and Cu(I)–citrate/glycinate complexes (no reliable data). Constants flagged "less certain" carry ±0.3–0.4 log units, i.e. ±0.1–0.2 pH or ±20 mV on the affected lines.

Scope. Pure thermodynamics at 25 °C: above the Cl₂/Cl⁻ line (+1.42 V) and in practice above ≈ +1.0 V the organic ligands and ammonia are not oxidatively stable; Fe(III)-citrate is also photoreducible. Kinetic facts of electrodeposition (overpotentials, anomalous codeposition) are outside the diagram. Atacamite Cu₂(OH)₃Cl and CuCl(s) were included as candidates and never become stable (0.1 M Cl⁻ is too low for CuCl(s) at 1 mM Cu; Cu(OH)₂ is the more stable hydroxide above pH 8.9).

Solver: fixed-point / Newton iteration on the four ligand mass balances with exact metal mass balances (all complexes mononuclear); converged to |Δlog T| < 1e-7 in 17 iterations over the whole grid. Data files with the constants and the boundary/junction records accompany this page.
